I would describe myself as such: a consequentialist,[^1] believing that outcomes are the ultimate factor in 'goodness', closer to Mill than Kant; contextualist,[^2] in that I am not a strict utilitarian and that 'right' depends on context; compatibilist,[^3] I believe that free will and determinism can logically coexist; empiricist,[^4][^5] in that I trust evidence gathered through the senses over intuition or authority, like Hume and Locke over Descartes; individualist,[^6] in that personal freedom should take priority over collective interest; libertarian-leaning,[^7] in that people should be free to keep what they earn provided it doesn't harm others; meritocratic,[^8] in that outcomes should track effort and ability rather than be redistributed for fairness's sake where no inherent disadvantages exist; existentialist,[^9] in that I believe meaning is something we create for ourselves rather than something inherent to life, agreeing with Sartre; progressive,[^10] in that I see human history as generally moving towards something better over time, agreeing with Condorcet rather than the declinists; and dualist-curious,[^11] in that I suspect the mind may be more than just the physical brain, despite my empiricist tendencies I have a soft spot for Descartes.
