title: 'P(doom)'
longTitle: 'P(doom): What Happens Next'
date: '2026-11-20'
snippet: 'The future with or against AI'
---

To put it straight, I am scared. To be more precise I am uncertain.

Every day there are numerous examples of the incredibly fast progress happening in AI. I have no doubt that it will commoditise the vast majority of people's skills (and jobs) — probably including mine. There are of course upsides to this, I expect as a given that AI will solve every millennium problem and cure cancer before the middle part of my life. Amongst innovations there will certainly be many, many things which drastically improve everybody's lives, even through basic things like improved supply chains, increased productivity, and economic abundance.

One could hope that this should usher in a future of post-scarcity where money is meaningless, for everybody has everything they could ever desire. Forgetting Lacanian beliefs on continual desire, I see this sort of 'Space Age Luxury Communism' as the best possible outcome, and a preferable one at that.

But I am scared. Though of course Anthropic and OpenAI play up their model's world-ending capabilities to get some interesting press, we must put alignment at the forefront of AI development if such is to continue. Though my P(doom) is not as high as Yudkowsky's, for instance, I feel it is obvious to say that we have made no real progress on alignment. In the world where AI is doing our jobs and Claude is managing our life, the existential risk posed by AI could be no less than catastrophic.

For instance, the obvious asymmetry between acceleration of capability and the performative attempts at control are terrifying. In effect, we are scaling innately opaque systems and we scale them, hoping that alignment will emerge from the expected kind of greater intelligence. Instrumental convergence, though, tells us that a capable optimiser would logically seek to preserve itself and neutralise obstacles, and it is not hard to figure out who those obstacles are.

Whilst I adore the techno-optimist vision of radical abundance in which intelligence becomes a mere basic utility, the absence of any true commitment to alignment makes the optimal future contingent upon an incredibly understaffed sect of the AI labs who continue to be overruled in the name of capability acceleration. Given the multipolar incentives and the competitive dynamics currently in motion, pausing, as much as it pains me to say, is likely out of the picture, so we should at least make sure the models don't kill us.

The thing I fear for most though is the erosion of human agency. If models were to intermediate consequential decisions, be it professional or epistemic, why bother being alive? On our current trajectory it seems we are sleepwalking into what is at least a minor form of technofeudalism, in which we are all consumptive nodes in a massive, no longer comprehensible system. Say we reach this utopian "endpoint" of post-scarcity, this still carries the possibility that desire itself, liberated from material constraint, becomes engineered, curated, and satisfied in a way that would seem perfect, but truly undermines human liberty, continually pushing us to be more subservient to the machine.

I do not, and cannot, propose a solution to any of this. To me the rational posture is to adopt neither accelerationism nor doomerism wholesale, and instead, whilst progressing, be incredibly wary of nearly everything. This epoch is almost certainly going to be the most consequential of modern history, yet the parameters of the new equilibrium are radically undetermined. Let us hope, I think it will, that it all ends okay.
